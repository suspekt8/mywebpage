Abstract and table of contents

This book offers an in-depth discussion of the features that characterize good scientific theories. Theoretical virtues include testability, empirical accuracy, consistency, unifying power, simplicity, and fertility. The book uses historical case studies to reconsider received views of these virtues and their role in theory choice and scientific realism.

  1. Introduction
  2. Theoretical virtues, truth, and the argument from simplicity
  3. Pessimism, base rates, and the no-virtue-coincidence argument
  4. Novel success and predictivism
  5. Theoretical fertility without novel success
  6. Ad hoc hypotheses and the argument from coherence
  7. Theoretical virtues as confidence boosters and the argument from choice
  8. Philosophy of science by historical means
  9. Conclusion

Epilogue: the demarcation problem.
